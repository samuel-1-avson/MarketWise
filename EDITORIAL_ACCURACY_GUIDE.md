# MARKETWISE — Editorial, source and safeguarding guide

## The mental model to teach

Financial markets and sports wagering are **not identical mechanisms**, but this is not a claim that market speculation is safe, predictable or a reliable way to earn money. Distinguish each system by (a) what object or claim exists; (b) economic role; (c) why participants interact; (d) price or settlement mechanism; (e) failure and risk modes. Explain uncertainty and potential harms clearly.

## Accuracy checklist

- **Forex:** Distinguish currency conversion from derivatives, exchange quote convention from actual transaction settlement, economic need from speculation. Avoid oversimplifying the global decentralized market.
- **Stocks:** Equity ownership does not promise dividends, control or gain; a share can lose value.
- **Commodities:** Physical products and financial contracts differ; prices respond to multiple factors and contracts have obligations/risks.
- **Crypto:** Token types differ; blockchains are not the only digital-ledger designs; not all tokens represent ownership in a business; custody and protocol risks matter.
- **Sports betting:** Discuss only the abstract mechanism of wagering on an event under operator rules, non-ownership, uncertainty, risk and potential harm. Do not provide bookmaker names, betting platforms, actionable odds or payout formulae, betting strategies, wager simulations, links or access instructions.
- **Comparisons:** A system's economic purpose is not a safety guarantee; analysis does not eliminate risk; genuine assets can still be the subject of harmful speculation.
- **Local examples:** Ghana (GHS imports, gold, cocoa) may provide familiar scenarios; avoid invented live exchange rates and unverified regulatory claims.

## Sources and citations

- Treat reference links in `PRODUCT_AND_BUILD_SPEC.md` as **candidate primary reading**; verify that pages remain available and accurately support claims before launch.
- Maintain `sourceId`, title, URL, publisher and review date. Connect specific claims/sections to sources; avoid a generic uncited list.
- Never fabricate citations, regulation dates, market figures, quotes or exact trading-hour claims.
- If external verification isn't possible in current environment, clearly mark `Needs editorial verification` rather than bluffing.
- Keep illustrative sample values flagged as **'Illustrative only — not live data'** directly beside diagrams/charts, plus a methodology note.

## Content and audience care

- Accessible, nonjudgmental language suitable for students and adults alike; define technical terms.
- No implied guarantees, personalized suggestions, performance ranking, hype, FOMO, 'make money fast', 'start wagering' or casino-style calls to action.
- Do not link to gambling services, referral programs, sportsbooks or methods of access.
- No gambling product instructions, pricing/payout tools or simulated bets.
- No brokerage integration, live order/purchase walkthrough, wallet connection or live funds transfer.
- When an example involves sensitive financial decisions, explain the risk instead of encouraging action.
- Explain that this is a learning resource and not individualized professional advice.

## Editor release audit

For every lesson and slide, ask: Is the core assertion clear? Does the example use synthetic data? Can readers see its limitations? Is the mechanism accurately distinguished from others? Is it accessible without animated visuals? Is the risk explicitly presented? Does each factual claim have a valid source? Can younger users consume it without being urged to gamble or trade? Is the material dated/reviewed appropriately?
