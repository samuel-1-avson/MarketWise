# Presentation and livestream design guide

## Goal

Create an **integrated 24-scene talk**, not a generic slideshow export. The core question is: **“Is forex sports betting in a suit?”** Explain that financial markets and wagering have differing objects, economic purposes, counterparties, risk channels and settlement structures while speculative behavior in either context can be risky. Be educational, not promotional.

## Run of show (suggested, flexible 40–60 minutes)

| Segment                | Slide range | Learning purpose                                   | Approximate pacing |
| ---------------------- | ----------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Hook and mental models | 1–4         | Establish the claim, risk and market language      | 5–8 min            |
| Forex                  | 5–8         | Exchange rates, importer example and leverage risk | 7–10 min           |
| Stocks                 | 9–10        | Equity ownership and expectations                  | 4–6 min            |
| Commodities            | 11–13       | Physical goods, contracts and supply shocks        | 5–8 min            |
| Crypto                 | 14–15       | Networks, ledger concepts and risks                | 4–7 min            |
| Sports comparison      | 16–18       | Mechanism differences and behavioral overlap       | 5–8 min            |
| Myths and synthesis    | 19–24       | Frictions, risk, knowledge checks and follow-up    | 9–13 min           |

All numbers represent **talk pacing**, not predictions or market data.

## Slide construction requirements

1. Use the exact 24 titles and intents from specification section 8, while improving compositions and originality.
2. Each slide has one main claim, one signature visual, a short legible stage headline and optional context line. Long detail belongs in notes, not tiny on-slide text.
3. Speaker notes contain: key teaching point; 2–4 steps of explanation; beginner analogy; misconception correction; transition; a source id where needed; optional audience question. Around 80–130 words per slide is a useful baseline, not a cap.
4. Presenter and public-view modes must not accidentally reveal speaker notes during a live broadcast. Provide deliberate notes controls; clear visible status.
5. Support a no-animation/reduced-motion presentation that still communicates every concept.
6. Interactive detours to labs open a clear return path to the precise slide and preserve timer/navigation context where possible.
7. A 'Start presentation' CTA opens the title slide, a slide TOC can jump chapters and a print-friendly handout exposes headings, explanations and source links.
8. Render legibly at 1280×720 and 1920×1080 with safe margins suitable for streaming overlays.

## Rehearsal checklist

- Open on the actual capture monitor/browser; verify 16:9 fit and non-obscured slide labels.
- Test every keyboard control and tap control; check full-screen and notes toggles.
- Click all planned lab demos, then use return-to-slide; ensure no external data failures.
- Check slide transitions at normal and reduced motion settings.
- Use offline-ready local synthetic demos; do not rely on real market prices.
- Verify QR/link points to the actual educational site only **after** explicit publishing approval.
- Keep local handout as a backup for live explanation.
- Make clear to the audience that everything is illustrative and not personalized finance advice.

## Post-stream publication

Use the web app as a self-paced knowledge resource. If the owner later elects to publish, validate all cited claims, privacy notices, browser support and accessibility; obtain approval for deployment. No gambling service links, referrals or trade-onboarding funnels should be added.
